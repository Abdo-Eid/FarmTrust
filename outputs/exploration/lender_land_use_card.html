The read, in four layers

Observed (near-fact)

Satellite greenness rose and fell 4 times in 2.1 years — two full cycles per year — each returning to bare soil. Backed by 248 clean cloud-free looks (89% of passes), 38–67 per season, typical gap 2–3 days.

Interpreted (likely)

The classic Nile-Delta winter → summer double-crop — a winter cereal/forage then summer corn. A worked, intensively-managed field; not fallow or abandoned.

Confidence (and why)

High it is actively double-cropped — every season is so densely observed a crop could not have hidden. Medium on crop identity (calendar timing + grower notes). None on yield or income.

Watch (caution)

Yield is unobserved; one corn season was pest-damaged but read healthy. Short 2-year record. A winter crop-character change. See the risk register below.

Four consecutive crop cycles, no missed season — the field is worked every season with a brief bare-soil turnover between, the signature of active management. The winter crop changed from a wheat+berseem mix (2024/25) to berseem-only (2025/26).

Track record

Reliability over time is what underwriting cares about. The satellite gives a 2-year baseline — long enough to establish the field's rhythm, not long enough to certify a multi-year trend.

2 of ~5 seasons toward a certifiable use-stability claim. Status so far: stable (consistent double-crop both years), with one change of character (winter mix → berseem-only). Treat stability as provisional until a longer record accrues.

Risk register — what to weigh

  • Yield is invisible to the satellite. The 2025 corn was hit by Fall Armyworm and the crop suffered, yet the canopy read healthy from orbit — canopy health ≠ harvested yield. The read confirms the land is worked, not that it produced well.
  • Short record. Two years establishes a rhythm but not a trend; a single bad year ahead could not yet be distinguished from normal variability.
  • Crop-character change. The winter crop shifted from a wheat+berseem mix to berseem-only — a move toward forage. Revenue implication is unknown from satellite alone.
  • Small parcel (~0.9 ha). Adequate here, but near the lower bound where 10 m pixels reliably resolve a single field.
  • No peer baseline yet. Without neighbouring fields we cannot say how this parcel performs relative to its area — the strongest control for distinguishing a field-specific problem from a bad year region-wide.

What this does NOT tell you

This is an objective, hard-to-fake confirmation that the collateral land is genuinely and consistently farmed — a complement to, not a substitute for, financial due-diligence. It is silent on:

  • Harvested yield or volume
  • Price, revenue, input costs, net income
  • Water rights or allocation
  • Ownership, title, or legal status
