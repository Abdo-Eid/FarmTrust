11 · MSAVI vs NDVI (quick test)

MSAVI2 is a soil-adjusted greenness index from the same red/NIR bands. Does it beat NDVI for this field?

Same story, sharper floor. NDVI and MSAVI2 track almost identically over time (r = 0.98) - same cycles, same timing - so for phenology and planting detection they are interchangeable. The difference is at the edges: MSAVI strips the soil glow, so between-crop troughs read ~0.10-0.14 (genuinely bare) vs NDVI's ~0.25, and it saturates less (top panels: same field, NDVI 0.79 vs MSAVI 0.52 at the corn peak), spreading dense and moderate canopy further apart. Net: keep NDVI as the familiar backbone; reach for MSAVI specifically for bare-soil / fallow / planting-onset detection. It does not fix the real ceiling - still no Fall Armyworm.

The two methods agree. The HMM learns four phenological states from the data alone — LOW (fallow), RISING, HIGH, DECLINING — and its activity windows land on the same four crop cycles, boundaries within a few days. A genuine cross-validation: an unsupervised probabilistic model with no 20%-amplitude rule independently reproduces the result, so the cycles are a property of the field, not an artefact of our threshold. HMM adds learned (not hand-tuned) thresholds and a per-day state probability (built-in uncertainty), and is well-cited for remote-sensing phenology; it needs a slope feature since RISING and DECLINING share the same NDVI level. The current approach keeps simplicity and directly-interpretable yield-proxy metrics. The lone 6-day sliver at the start is the previous winter crop's tail at the record edge, not a real extra cycle.
